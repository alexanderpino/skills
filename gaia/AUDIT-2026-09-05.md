## 1. The tooling

`STATE.md` asks: *"can I make this green on a broken corpus?"* Yes, in two ways that matter and
several that do not. All reproduced against a scratch copy; the tree was left clean.

### T1 — HIGH · Deleting `status:` produces a document the index calls **stable** and the guard exempts from `verified:`

Three defaults disagree about a missing key:

| Site | Code | Effect of a missing `status:` |
|---|---|---|
| `check.py:202` | `fm.get("status", "stable")` | validates — the default is `stable` |
| `check.py:220` | `fm.get("status") == "stable"` | `None != "stable"`, so the *"stable needs `verified:`"* rule never fires |
| `index.py:77` | `fm.get("status", "stable")` | the Status column prints **stable** |

Reproduced end to end: remove the `status: draft` line from `references/caustics.md`, run
`index.py`, run `check.py` → **exit 0**, and `index.md` now advertises

```
| [`caustics.md`](caustics.md) | Underwater and surface caustics on terrain: … | stable |
```

against an index banner that calls the `status` column "the whole point".

This is the one hole that erases the line `SKILL.md` draws between attribution and verification,
and it is reachable by **deleting** a line rather than adding one — which is how it would actually
happen, in a paste or a merge. Everything else in the guard is built to resist an author who is
trying; this needs only an author who is careless.

**Fix (one line each):** default to `draft` in both files, or make an absent `status` a problem.
Then add a mutation row to the `bites` job: *delete `status:`, regenerate the index, guard must
go red.* Note that the existing `illegal status value` mutation does **not** cover this — it
tests a wrong value, not an absent key.

### T2 — HIGH · `verified:` certifies a citation set, not a claim

`sources_digest()` hashes only sorted `(id, locator)` pairs. Its docstring's promise is true —
"adding or re-pointing a citation invalidates it automatically" — and insufficient, because the
stamp means *a human read the cited work and it says what the document claims*, and the claims are
outside the digest.

Reproduced: stamp `caustics.md` with a well-formed
`verified: - { by: human:<id>, at: …, covers: <its own digest> }`, set `status: stable`, then
replace **every prose sentence in the body** with an invention ("Caustics are produced by the
terrain's magnetic field and scale with the cube of the moon's albedo"), preserving only headings,
tables and citation markers. `check.py` → **exit 0**; the index prints **checked**.

Latent today — no document carries `verified:` — which is precisely when it is cheap to fix, and
before the first stamp lands is the only moment the fix costs nothing.

**Fix:** fold a hash of the body into the digest. Hashing the whole body makes the stamp brittle
against a typo fix; hashing `## Use this` plus the failure table covers the two places a reader
lands and the two places this corpus records its corrections landing. Either is better than
hashing neither.

### T3 — MEDIUM · The fabrication surface is unguarded, and one comment overstates the cover

`check.py:265-271` reads: *"a marker matching NOTHING ANYWHERE — a fabricated citation, the single
worst thing this skill can ship — was dropped in silence. An unknown marker is now the loudest
failure here."*

Reproduced: append a wholly invented entry to `papers-rendering.md` —

```
- **vandermeer2019** `P` — van der Meer, Q. & Solis, T. (2019). *Spectral caustic transport on
  displaced heightfields.* ACM TOG 38(4).
```

— declare it in `caustics.md` with locator `"§4.2 eq. 11, the transport kernel"`, cite it in the
body. **Green.** It also counts as a *sharp* locator and raises the `locators` ratio.

The check catches a marker with no *entry*. Adding the entry defeats it, and the entry is
unverified prose written by the same author as the claim. `SKILL.md` is honest about this at the
corpus level ("the same kind of author writes the claim, the citation and this guard"); the comment
inside the guard is not, and it is the comment a maintainer reads. `requote.py` is the only
instrument aimed at this and reaches 6 of 119 quotations.

**Fix:** reword the comment to what the check actually proves — *a marker resolves to a declared
entry* — and record the fabrication surface as an OPEN row in `guard-proofs.tsv` beside the two
already there.

### T5 — LOW · `check_propagation` reads a key that never exists


`_scan()` builds entries with exactly `{tier, ref, background, no_artefact, not_opened, file}`.

### T7 — LOW · Dead conditions, and an uncapped bibliography


Both trailing conditions are unreachable: `skip` already excluded those paths at the top of the

*"**N have been checked by a human**; the rest are `draft`"* — untrue for a `deprecated` document,

### What the tooling gets right

Worth saying plainly, because the findings above are the short list and this is the long one.

- The `bites` job is real mutation testing, not a smoke test: **fifteen** mutations that must turn
  the guard red, two more that must break `--selftest`, one that must stale the index, and **two**
  decoys that must leave the guard green — run against a copy, each mutation **asserting it
  applied** so a no-op sed cannot make the job vacuous. Several exist because a reviewer defeated
  the previous implementation, and the job says so in the comment.
- `--selftest` gives every *reported* metric a fixture set, on the correct reasoning that an
  assertion fails for a living but a number does not.
- `check_section_reach` ships a **measured negative result in its own docstring** — it does not
  catch the case it was built for, with the reason (lexical overlap cannot see a semantic gap) and
  the evidence (the exact document and commit). I do not know another codebase that does this.
- `okf.py` rejects rather than guesses, and its comments record the specific silent corruptions
  each rule exists to prevent — the apostrophe that merged three tags into one and refiled a
  document, `[a, b` silently dropping an element, the Unicode line separators.
- The `no artefact:` marker is *earned* against a structural tag in another file, after a reviewer
  showed the previous prose-regex form could be beaten by moving four words onto line 1.

### C1 — MEDIUM-HIGH · `water-optics.md`: `c` is not "5–20×" `K_d` in the water this document is built around

> *"Because natural water scatters strongly forward, `c` typically runs **5–20x larger** than
> `K_d`. Whichever of the two a single constant was fitted to, the other term is wrong by that
> factor."*

The ratio is set by single-scattering albedo, not by "natural water". With `c = a + b` and
Gordon's `K_d ≈ (a + b_b)/μ_d`, `μ_d ≈ 0.75`:

```
c/K_d  =  μ_d · (a + b) / (a + b_b)      →  μ_d ≈ 0.75   as b → 0   (pure absorption)
                                         →  μ_d / B ≈ 42  as b ≫ a  (B = b_b/b ≈ 0.018, particulate)
```

Computed with **this document's own recommended absorption** (Pope & Fry) and standard molecular
scattering `b_w(500) = 0.0029 m⁻¹ ∝ λ^-4.32`:

| Water | c/K_d |
|---|---|
| pure water, 450 / 500 / 550 / 610 nm | **0.90 / 0.80 / 0.76 / 0.75** |
| Jerlov I (clearest ocean) | 0.92 |
| clear oceanic | 1.4 |
| coastal | 4.1 |
| turbid | 7.0 |
| very turbid | 17.9 |

So 5–20× is a **turbid-water** range presented as universal, in a document whose `## Use this`
is built on pure-water absorption and whose worked examples are a clear lake and an ocean. In pure
water the inequality *reverses*: `K_d > c`, because `K_d ≥ a/μ̄ > a ≈ c` once scattering is
negligible. A reader who applies the stated factor to an alpine lake makes the beam path roughly
six times too attenuating — and the failure row *"Water looks far murkier than it is … `c` and
`K_d` are two coefficients, 5–20x apart"* points them at the same number.

**Fix:** keep the doctrine — two coefficients, two transport paths, export both — and replace the
bare range with the mechanism and its span: *the ratio runs from about 0.75 in pure water, where
`K_d` exceeds `c`, to tens in particle-laden water; it is governed by the single-scattering albedo,
so take it from the body's own `a` and `b`, never from a rule of thumb.* That also removes the
"wrong by that factor" sentence, which inherits the error.

**Everything else in this document reproduced to the printed digit**, which makes the one failure
easier to trust rather than harder:

| Claim | Document | Reproduced |
|---|---|---|
| cosine-weighted `R_ext`, `R_int`, ratio | 6.67% / 47.63% / 7.14× | **6.672% / 47.634% / 7.139×** |
| `R_ext` at normal / at 60° | 2.06% / 6.01% | **2.058% / 6.007%** |
| Schlick signed error over 38–79° | max +14.3% @ 78.89°, min −22.8% @ 51.3°, mean −8.9° | **+14.32% @ 78.89°, −22.75% @ 51.30°, −8.91%** |
| Schlick at 83.8° | +11.4% | **+11.40%** |
| θ_c, cos²θ_c, 1/cos θ_c at n = 1.335 | 48.51° / 0.4389 / 1.5094 | **48.5093° / 0.4389 / 1.5094** |
| Snell's window, three fractions | 97° / 33.8% / 56.1% / 43.9% | **97.02° / 0.3375 / 0.5611 / 0.4389** |
| `F0` at 1.335, and the 1.31–1.47 span | 0.0206, 0.018–0.036 | **0.02058, 0.0180–0.0362** |
| refracted-path overstatement, 7 angles | 1.6 / 7.1 / 20.0 / 52.2 / 167 / 664 / 3700 % | **all seven, to the printed digit** |
| Lee 2015 eq. 33 prefactor at three `R_rs` | 0.95 / 0.85 / 0.77 | **0.948 / 0.854 / 0.774** |
| Pope & Fry `a(700)/a(417.5)` | 141× | **141.8×** |

Two further things deserve credit. The absorption triples (`0.2644 / 0.0565 / 0.00922` at
610/550/450 nm, and `0.2755 / 0.0511 / 0.00979` at 620/545/460) are the genuine tabulated Pope &
Fry values, and the Smith & Baker "about 3.4× too high at 420 nm" is right — in a document whose
locator openly declares the paper was never opened. And the discipline the document imposes on
itself — one `n` per document, sample wavelengths quoted with the constant, "say which weighting"
on both the Fresnel integrals and Snell's window — is exactly the discipline whose absence
produces the disagreements it is written to prevent.

### C2 — MEDIUM · `shallow-water.md`: the mass update names an undefined symbol, and the natural reading destroys the section's own claim

```
  Σf  = sum(f_i)
  K   = (Σf > 0) ? min(1, h * cellArea / (dt * Σf)) : 1
  f_i *= K
  h  += dt * (inflow - outflow) / cellArea            # <-- `outflow` is never defined
```

`Σf` is the **pre-clamp** sum. `outflow` appears nowhere else in the document. The correct value is
the **post-clamp** sum, `K·Σf`; reusing the only defined symbol is what an implementer does, and it
breaks *"the outflow scaling factor `K` makes positivity unconditional"* outright.

Evidence — I hit it on the first transcription, which is the point:

| Reading of the last line | min depth over 400 steps, C = 0.2 … 20 | relative mass drift |
|---|---|---|
| `outflow = Σf` (pre-clamp — the natural reading) | **−5.6 × 10¹⁷⁸**, then NaN | 3.9 × 10¹⁷⁸ |
| `outflow = K·Σf` (post-clamp — intended) | **−0.000000** | **1.11 × 10⁻¹⁶** |

The second row is the document's own printed result, reproduced exactly, including
`≤ 1.1e-16`. So the *claim* is right and the *block* under-specifies it, in the one document that
calls the missing clamp "the single most common port error". One word fixes it: recompute `Σf`
after the scale, or write `h += dt * (inflow - K*Σf) / cellArea`.

Every other measured claim in this document also reproduced independently:

| Claim | Document | Reproduced |
|---|---|---|
| `dt_crit`, constant `A`, depths 0.1 / 1 / 10 | 0.1604 flat | **0.1602 flat** |
| `dt_crit`, `A = h·lx` | 0.5056 / 0.1599 / 0.0506 | **0.5049 / 0.1597 / 0.0505** |
| `dt_crit / (dx/√(gA/l))` across `A, l, dx` | 0.502 | **0.5019, invariant over all 8 combinations** |
| `K` NaN on a mostly-dry grid | 63 of 64 at step 0, whole field after one step | **63 of 64; 64 of 64 after one step** |
| `min(1, NaN)` vs `min(NaN, 1)` in scalar Python | 1 / NaN | **1 / nan** |
| `√(g·h)` at 1 mm | 0.099 m/s | **0.09905 m/s** |

The signal-speed derivation is also correct and is the sharpest thing in the document: linearising
the block gives `c² = A·g·dx²/(l·lx·ly)`, i.e. `√(g·A/l)` on a square grid, so `A/l` is the model's
**effective depth** and Šťava's `A = l²` makes it one cell — the reason a per-step `max(√(gh))`
reduction over a constant-`A` pipe grid measures nothing. The measured flat row and the
1/√h row confirm both branches.

### C3 — LOW · `planetary-precision.md`: the ULP-bound sentence is inverted

> *"…not the `x · 1.2e-7` upper bound — that bound is the spacing just under a power of two and
> runs up to 2× high everywhere else"*

Measured `bound/exact`:

| x | bound / exact |
|---|---|
| 2¹⁹ · 1.0001 (just **above** a power of two) | **1.0001** |
| mid-binade | 1.4000 |
| 2²⁰ · 0.9999 (just **under** the next) | **1.9998** |

The bound is tight just *above* a power of two and 2× high just *under* the next. The sentence
swaps the tight and loose ends — in the paragraph whose entire purpose is to be more careful than
the bound. Everything else in the table is exact: **0.061 mm / 0.98 mm / 7.8 mm / 6.25 cm / 0.5 m**
at 1 / 10 / 100 / 1000 / 6371 km, and the "still 6.25 cm at 900 km, doubling at 2²⁰ ≈ 1049 km"
follow-up, all confirmed.

### C4 — LOW · `planetary-precision.md`: a cross-document consistency claim that is itself inconsistent

### C5 — LOW · `simulation-time-budget.md` contradicts `shallow-water.md` in one sentence

> *"An ambient ocean has no timestep and no state; that is why it fits in a frame and a pipe model
> does not."*

`shallow-water.md` recommends the pipe form as *"the default at both time budgets — the same
machinery drives a generator's hydraulic erosion offline and an engine's interactive patch per
frame"*. And `simulation-time-budget.md`'s own decision table files *"a filling pool"* under
Runtime and calls explicit adaptive substepping *"the answer for nearly all runtime water"*. The
intended reading is presumably *an ocean-sized* pipe model; as written it is a flat contradiction
of both its sibling and itself.

### C6 — LOW · `heightfield-lod.md`: `rho = e·K/d` is a worst-case bound presented as the projected error

Geometric error `e` is **vertical**; its screen-space projection carries a factor depending on the
angle between the error direction and the view ray, and goes to zero looking straight down. Using
the orientation-independent bound is the right engineering call — it is conservative and cheap —
but the document presents it as *the* projected error. A reader who instruments it and finds
measured error consistently below prediction has no way to tell whether the metric is conservative
or broken, and the document's own diagnostic (*"halving `tau` should roughly quadruple triangle
count"*) is the thing they would then distrust. One sentence.

### S2 — LOW · Bibliography count drift

The generated index says **"37 document(s), plus 7 bibliographies"**. `STATE.md:18` says *"plus six
bibliographies"* and `README.md:121` says *"37 documents + 6 bibliographies"*. There are seven
`papers-*.md` files. Both wrong files are the hand-written ones with no guard — which is exactly
what `STATE.md`'s closing paragraph predicts of itself, so the process is working; the numbers

### S3 — Observation · Trigger coverage sits on the guard's floor

62 queries, 46 positive, 16 negative. `check_trigger_coverage` requires every `covered` document to
be reached by at least one positive query, and **32 of 37 are reached by exactly one**. The suite
reads thorough at 62 rows and is near-minimal in reach. The 16 negatives are genuinely adversarial
and well chosen (GIS reprojection, FLIP smoke, GGX, GAN heightmap synthesis, navmesh A*, Bazel) —
they test the boundaries `SKILL.md`'s *When not to use this* actually draws.

### S4 — Observation · The evals are behaviour-axis only

19 evals over `{recommendation, tier-discipline, time-budget, trap-resistance, verification-honesty}`
— a defensible design, since those are the behaviours the doctrine asserts. But no eval is
organised by subject axis, and the CI shape check asserts `axis` exists without constraining its
values, so a typo creates a new axis silently. Rendering and Architecture carry 18 of 37 documents
between them and no eval names either.

### What the structure gets right

`STATE.md`'s own arithmetic is **exact**: 37 documents = 10 + 1 + 5 audited + 21 unexamined; the
coverage totals, the bibliography counts, and all five reported metrics match `check.py`'s live

three documents had drifted, and `check_headings` existing because a splice-by-string-replace
