# Closed water and open water are two different problems

Read this before the wave model, before the solver and before the shader. A pool and a sea share
an interface, a set of optical coefficients and a pass order — and share almost nothing else. Ship
one water system with a "size" parameter and you will spend the project fighting defaults that were
correct for the other body.

## Use this

**Carry an authored `bodyType` on every body, and gate machinery off it.** Not a size threshold, not
a heuristic — a value an author sets, because the distinction is not measurable from the mesh:

```
bodyType = sea | lake | pond | pool | tank | basin | canal | reservoir | river
```

A terrain generator can classify *lake versus river* from a fill mask and flow accumulation. **No
classifier turns a gunite shell into a lake**, so man-made bodies arrive authored and extend the
enum on the engine side [bodytype_doctrine].

**The two questions that decide everything downstream are fetch and boundary.** Everything in the
gate table below follows from one of them.

## Fetch: why a pool has no waves of its own

Fetch is the distance wind blows unobstructed over water. It sets the wave energy a body can hold,
and it is *not* a smooth dial — there is a floor below which wind cannot raise a gravity wave at
all.

Including surface tension, the dispersion relation has a **minimum** phase speed
[capillary_gravity]:

```
sigma = 0.0728 N/m, rho = 1000 kg/m^3, g = 9.81 m/s^2
c_min   = (4 g sigma / rho)^(1/4)   = 0.2312 m/s
lam_min = 2 pi sqrt(sigma / (rho g)) = 0.01712 m       # 1.712 cm
```

⚠️ **Declare `sigma` once and derive both halves.** The pair `23.1 cm/s at 1.73 cm` circulates
widely and is internally inconsistent — those two numbers imply surface tensions 2.5% apart. Each
is defensible alone, which is exactly why the mismatch survives being checked. Quote a pair only
from one constant.

Wind cannot force a wave until it can push past `c_min`, which is why a breeze produces *patches*
of ripple separated by glassy water rather than uniform texture. And over metres of fetch nothing
larger ever grows. So:

- **Open body (sea, large lake).** A fetch-limited or fully-developed wind sea, plus **swell**
  generated by weather thousands of kilometres away. It never goes truly flat: a glassy ocean still
  breathes on a long, low-amplitude undulation, and killing all displacement at low wind gives a
  rigid sheet with moored boats frozen in it.
- **Closed body (pool, tank, basin).** The wind-driven part collapses onto the capillary–gravity
  floor and becomes the *smallest* term. The waves come from **the plumbing and the walls**.

⚠️ **Swell on a mountain tarn is the classic misclassification tell.** A lake has no distant storm
feeding it, so its dead calm really is a mirror and its only waves are local. If your water system
puts residual swell on every body, it has no `bodyType`.

## Boundary: the sim edge is a wall, or it is a lie

The second question, and the one that produces the most structural bugs.

- **Open water**: a simulated patch is a *window* onto a body that continues past it. Its edge is
  an artefact and must be hidden — fade the sim's contribution to zero over the outer ~15% of the
  domain. A hard line where wakes stop existing is one of the most-reported water artefacts.
- **Closed water**: the domain edge is a **real wall**, and it reflects. The contract is inverted.
  Fading a pool's waves out at the tiles is drawing the sim's domain, not the water.

Keep the fade *only* where a sim domain genuinely ends inside a larger body — a patch around the
player in a lake has both: reflecting shore, fading offshore edge.

## The wave field in a closed body is a driven basin

⚠️ **"Driven basin" is a phrase this family of skills coined; it is not a term of art**, and
`bodytype_doctrine` has **no external source** [bodytype_doctrine] — it is a convention this
repository recommends, not a finding. The construction under the name is borrowed from room
acoustics (a point source, early reflections, a diffuse tail) and the physics inside it is the
ordinary capillary–gravity kind; the *name* and the *packaging* are ours. Use it as a mental model;
never cite it as literature.

A directional wind spectrum on a sheltered pool is plausible and wrong in a way a photograph
exposes immediately. Pool water is organised by the plumbing and the walls
[bodytype_doctrine]:

- **The source is a fixed point** — the filtration return jets, injecting a narrow band of gravity
  waves of order 10–30 cm whenever the pump runs, with swimmer transients on top.
- **The walls send it all back.** ⚠️ That a tiled wall is a near-total reflector at these
  wavelengths is *argued from the physics*: **no reflection coefficient has been measured**, here or
  in the doctrine this comes from, and the reverberant picture rests on that unmeasured assumption.
  Granting it, the result is a reverberant response: the field is **not statistically homogeneous**,
  the pattern is **stationary in the basin frame** (the same structure sits in the same place every
  day), and a train can be traced from the inlet to the far wall and back. A spectral ocean model is
  homogeneous and stationary in *time*; it is the wrong statistics.
- **Damping sorts the surface into two bands, and forcing sorts it the same way.** Deep-water
  viscous decay goes as `k^2` [lamb_damping], so the e-folding distance against group speed is
  order 90 m at 16.5 cm — eleven lengths of an 8 m pool — but only about 2 m at 3 cm, which dies
  before the far wall.

| | Long band (≳10 cm) | Short band (≲5 cm) |
|---|---|---|
| Source | Return jets, swimmers — a fixed point | Wind, over the whole surface |
| Structure | Coherent, reverberant, stationary in the basin frame | Incoherent, statistically homogeneous |
| Reach | Rings the basin many times | Dies in ~2 m; never reflects |
| Carries | The visible undulation and most of the slope | Sparkle and fine bed texture |
| A lee or wind shadow | **Passes straight through** | **Removed** |

That last row is a cheap, high-value correctness test: in the wind shadow of a sail or a hedge the
surface goes glassy *but keeps undulating*. Multiply a lee mask into the whole field and long waves
stop dead at the shadow line, which no water does.

**Do not budget the two bands by wave height.** Shading sees slope, and a single wave's slope is
`2*pi*a/lambda` — equal slope costs amplitude proportional to wavelength, so a millimetre ripple
out-slopes a much taller long wave.

## The gate table

Every row is a default that flips [bodytype_doctrine]. ⚠️ **The table is doctrine, and doctrine with
no external source**: it is a set of engineering defaults this repository recommends, reasoned from
fetch and boundary, not a measured or published classification. The two questions it descends from
are physics; the enum, the rows, and the decision to gate on an *authored* type rather than on
geometry are a convention, and should be argued with rather than cited.

| Machinery | Open body | Closed body |
|---|---|---|
| Ambient wave spectrum | Fetch-limited wind sea, plus swell | Collapses to the capillary floor; the driven basin replaces it |
| Residual swell at zero wind | **On** — the sea always breathes | **Off** — dead calm is a mirror |
| Shoaling, refraction, breakers, run-up | The production default near shore | **Off.** No sloping bed, no surf zone. A pool with breakers is an ungated body type |
| Whitecaps (fold-driven foam) | From Force 3 up | **Off.** No fetch reaches the breaking threshold across 10 m |
| Shoreline foam, wet sand band | The strongest shoreline cue there is | **Degenerate** — the waterline is a hard edge on a vertical wall; a static wet band and a meniscus replace them |
| Sim-patch edge | Fade to zero over the outer ~15% | **Reflect.** The edge is a wall |
| Depth ramp | The single strongest realism cue water has | 1–3 m of range; almost no dynamic range to spend |
| What reads instead | The surface | The **bed** — wall/floor junction, the refracted straight-line grid, caustics |
| Reflection | Planar is a hero-body luxury | One small flat body: planar is genuinely affordable, and screen-space reflection behaves unusually well |
| Caustics | A detail on the bed | The dominant visual event |
| Stacked surfaces (water under a bridge and in a cave below) | A real architectural constraint | A non-issue — one surface, nothing stacked |

**The net effect is an inverted budget.** On an ocean you spend on the surface and economise on the
bottom. On a pool you spend on the bottom — caustics, bed albedo, refraction fidelity — and the
surface is a nearly flat sheet with ripples on it.

## Open water has an energy ladder; closed water does not

For an open body, wind speed is the single parameter that should drive the wave spectrum, the
whitecap coverage, the slope variance the sun glitter reads, the spray rate and the foam streak
direction. Wiring them separately is how you get a mirror-calm sea covered in foam, or a gale with
a needle-sharp sun highlight — both instantly wrong and both common.

The Beaufort scale is the right spine because its descriptors are *visual observations*, so they
map onto feature gates directly [beaufort]. Three transitions are observational facts rather than
art direction, and are worth hard-coding:

| Gate | Turns on |
|---|---|
| **Force 3** (7–10 kt) | Whitecaps — "crests begin to break, scattered whitecaps" |
| **Force 5** (17–21 kt) | Spray |
| **Force 7** (28–33 kt) | Streaked, wind-advected foam; orientation along wind starts to matter |

None of these gates ever fires on a closed body, which is the practical content of "a pool has no
sea state".

**What it beats.** *One water system with a size parameter* — the failure this document exists to
prevent; the defaults are structurally wrong for the other body, not wrong by a tuning margin.
*Classifying `bodyType` from geometry* — works for lake-versus-river from a fill mask, cannot
distinguish a reservoir from a lake or a tank from a pond, and silently mis-gates when it guesses.
*Per-body hand-tuned presets with no enum* — the same gates, discovered separately by each artist,
with no place to write down why.

## How this fails, and what it looks like

| Symptom | Mechanism | Fix |
|---|---|---|
| A mountain tarn heaves on a long swell | Residual swell applied without a `bodyType` gate | Gate swell to open bodies |
| A pool has whitecaps, or breakers at the tiles | Surf-zone and fold-foam machinery ungated | Gate on `bodyType`, not on wave height |
| Pool waves fade out before reaching the wall | Open-water edge fade applied to a closed domain | Reflect at a real wall; fade only where a patch ends inside a larger body |
| Wakes stop at an invisible line offshore | Missing edge fade on an open patch | Fade the outer ~15% |
| Pool surface is statistically uniform and reads as ocean-in-a-box | A spectrum used where a driven basin belongs | Point source plus reflecting walls; keep the pattern basin-stationary |
| Long waves stop dead at the edge of a wind shadow | A lee mask multiplied into the whole field | Modulate the short band only |
| Dead-calm water reads as patent leather or a black void with one searing highlight | Slope variance driven to zero | Clamp the variance to the solar-disc scale; spend the freed budget on reflection fidelity |
| Foam on a mirror-calm sea, or a gale with a pinpoint sun | Wind wired separately into each consumer | One wind, every consumer |
| Fine ripple octaves added to "sharpen" calm water, and it aliases | Sub-pixel detail past the aliasing budget; those wavelengths also die within centimetres to viscous damping | Cut on damping or on footprint — `lam_min` is where phase speed is slowest, not a shortest wave [lamb_damping] |
| The pool's bed looks flat and cheap while the surface is lavish | Open-water budget applied to a closed body | Invert it — the bed is the show |
