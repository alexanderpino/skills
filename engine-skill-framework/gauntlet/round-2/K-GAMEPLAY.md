# K-GAMEPLAY · Game Systems & Content Critic · G1 round 2

Scope: animation, audio, AI/nav/crowds, gameplay framework and toolkit, movement, gameplay data, narrative, scripting, input, UI, text, localization, accessibility, cinematics, camera/views, persistence/settings. `check.py` is green (0 errors, 0 warnings). None of the findings below can be seen by the gate.

### K-GAMEPLAY-1 · major · missing-contract
- Target: C-A11YRT, accessibility, audio-content-runtime, narrative-dialogue, cinematics-sequencer, input-system, post-color-hdr, gameplay-systems-toolkit, gameplay-architect, UI.A11Y.subtitles, UI.A11Y.motion, UI.A11Y.assists
- Finding: `C-A11YRT` (caption/subtitle submission, screen-reader announcements, accessibility settings and change events) has exactly one consumer: `ui-architect`. No skill that produces captions submits through it. Audio events have no route in, sequencer-timed cinematic lines have no route in (`cinematics-sequencer` consumes `C-DIALOGUE?`/`C-UI?` but not `C-A11YRT`), and gameplay has no way to make screen-reader announcements. Nothing that should react to accessibility settings subscribes to them either: `input-system` (hold/toggle, one-switch), `post-color-hdr` (colorblind/contrast), the toolkit camera (shake, FOV, head-bob under UI.A11Y.motion) and `gameplay-architect` (UI.A11Y.assists). Each of these consumes only the process contract `C-A11Y`, which is a requirements register and carries no runtime data. "Accessibility built in" therefore has no runtime path. Every agent will read a11y settings ad hoc, through C-CFG or its own options, or not at all.
- Evidence: XAG 101/104/107/112 and CVAA/EAA need settings that apply engine-wide at the moment they change, and captions that come from every audio source, not only dialogue. In production, the TLOU2 and Forza a11y suites route captions from the audio event system and cinematics through one caption service, and one settings service drives camera, input and post.
- Proposed change: add `C-A11YRT` to `consumes` of audio-content-runtime (caption submission for tagged audio events and VO timing), cinematics-sequencer, narrative-dialogue (optional), gameplay-architect (announcements, assists), gameplay-systems-toolkit (motion options for camera and shake), input-system (input assist settings) and post-color-hdr (as `C-A11YRT?`). Extend the `C-A11YRT` summary with "caption sources: dialogue, audio-event captions, sequencer; settings are read through a snapshot like C-CFG". Add a check that fails when any skill contributes to a `UI.A11Y.*` capability without consuming `C-A11YRT`.

### K-GAMEPLAY-2 · major · missing-contract
- Target: gameplay-systems-toolkit, crowd-simulation, cinematics-sequencer, C-GAME, C-ANIM, ai-behavior-perception, gameplay-architect
- Finding: three gameplay-facing experts provide no contract, and the lead contract they would sit behind does not describe their surface. `C-GAME` covers only "module entry points, rules/session state, control binding, scheduled systems, extension hooks". Abilities/effects/attributes, gameplay tags, spawning, volumes/markers and hit resolution (gameplay-systems-toolkit), crowd spawn/query/LOD (crowd-simulation), and sequence playback/binding/event tracks (cinematics-sequencer, whose `C-ANIM` covers only pose output) all have no contract. So:
  - AI cannot activate abilities or query tags. Behavior-tree tasks and state-tree conditions on tags and abilities are the standard pattern.
  - UI view-models cannot bind attributes.
  - Narrative conditions cannot test tags.
  - Game code cannot start or bind a cutscene or receive its events.
  - Nothing can drive a crowd.
  
  Under the "contracts, never internals" rule (00 §3), the only way to reach these surfaces is through internals.
- Evidence: UE GameplayAbilities and GameplayTags are consumed by AI (StateTree and BT tag decorators), UI (attribute binding) and animation. UE Mass exposes crowd spawning and queries through processors and subsystems. Level Sequence exposes a player/binding API to gameplay. None of this is internal-only.
- Proposed change: add `C-ABILITY` (L4, owner gameplay-systems-toolkit: abilities, effects, attributes, tag queries, messages, hit results), `C-CROWD` (L4, owner crowd-simulation: agent spawn/despawn, batched queries, LOD and tier hooks) and `C-SEQ` (L3, owner cinematics-sequencer: sequence playback, actor binding, event and track callbacks, skip/blend policy). Wire consumers: ai-behavior-perception → `C-ABILITY?`; gameplay-architect → `C-SEQ?`, `C-CROWD?`; narrative-dialogue conditions → tags via `C-GAMEDATA` or a new L3 `C-TAGS`. Tags must live at L3 if narrative (L3) is to read them.

### K-GAMEPLAY-3 · major · missing-contract
- Target: C-AIAGENT, ai-behavior-perception, C-MOVE, navigation-pathfinding (GAM.AI.following), crowd-simulation, character-movement
- Finding: `C-AIAGENT` is described as "optional decision providers (learned policies, LLM agents)". The established AI surface has no contract: behavior/state-tree assignment, perception stimulus registration (noise, sight sources that gameplay must emit), smart-object claim/release, and EQS-style queries. Movement is also broken. Nav owns "path following & steering" at L3, but it cannot consume `C-MOVE` (L4), and `ai-behavior-perception` does not consume `C-MOVE` either. So the framework has no path from a computed path to character locomotion: the only `C-MOVE` consumer is motion-synthesis. NPC locomotion through the same predicted and reconciled movement API as players (GAM.MOVE.networked) is therefore unreachable.
- Evidence: UE's AIController → PathFollowingComponent → CharacterMovement `RequestDirectMove`. Mass/StateTree smart-object claims. Perception stimuli are registered by gameplay objects (UE `AIPerceptionStimuliSource`). All of these are cross-skill surfaces.
- Proposed change: rename `C-AIAGENT` to "AI agents & decision providers" and extend it with brain assignment, stimulus sources, smart-object claims, query service, and the optional learned/LLM providers as one sub-part. Add `C-MOVE` to ai-behavior-perception `consumes` (optional in the minimal profile). State in the `C-NAV` summary that nav produces path corridors and steering intents and does not move bodies, and that the consumer converts intents to `C-MOVE` requests.

### K-GAMEPLAY-4 · major · missing-contract
- Target: C-SCRIPT, scripting-runtime, gameplay-architect, ui-architect (UI.FW.logic), ai-behavior-perception, narrative-dialogue, cinematics-sequencer, gameplay-systems-toolkit (GAM.SYS.volumes)
- Finding: `C-SCRIPT` has one consumer, modding-ugc. Designers author mostly through scripting and visual scripting, yet `C-GAME` has no way to register script-implemented systems, components or rules. Script systems also need declared data access to take part in the task-graph schedule (GAM.FW.execution, GAM.SCR.concurrency "deferred world mutation"), and scripting-runtime does not consume `C-FRAME`, `C-ID` or `C-ECS`. UI logic hosting (UI.FW.logic, contributor scripting-runtime), BT script tasks, Ink/Yarn external functions, sequencer event tracks and trigger-volume logic (contributor scripting-runtime) have no contract edge to scripting at all. The designer authoring path is therefore unowned at the integration seam.
- Evidence: every modern engine with scripting has an explicit script↔framework seam: Unity DOTS SystemBase/ISystem codegen, Roblox/Luau with deferred events and parallel Luau actors, and UE Verse/Blueprint through the reflection VM plus scheduling rules. Without it, scripts turn into per-object `Tick()` callbacks, which is the legacy pattern that 00 §6 rejects.
- Proposed change: add `C-SCRIPT` (optional) to `consumes` of gameplay-architect, ui-architect, ai-behavior-perception, narrative-dialogue, cinematics-sequencer and gameplay-systems-toolkit. Extend `C-SCRIPT` with "script-authored systems declare access sets, are scheduled through C-FRAME, and mutate the world only through deferred command buffers". Add `C-FRAME`, `C-ID` and `C-ECS?` to scripting-runtime `consumes`, and `C-DET?` (see K-GAMEPLAY-7).

### K-GAMEPLAY-5 · major · overlap
- Target: GAM.SAVE.settings, CORE.LIFE.config (C-CFG "user" source), C-SAVE, C-A11YRT (settings), input-system, accessibility, runtime-scalability
- Finding: player settings have two homes. `C-CFG` has a "user" configuration layer owned by core-runtime-architect. `GAM.SAVE.settings` (persistence-save) owns "player settings/options model, scopes (device/user/cloud) & first-boot availability". `C-A11YRT` separately owns "accessibility settings and change events". The `C-SAVE` summary does not mention settings. None of the three contributors to GAM.SAVE.settings (input-system for bindings, accessibility, runtime-scalability for graphics) consume `C-SAVE`, so none of them can register a setting. The result will be three persistence paths for options, with different precedence and different first-boot behavior.
- Evidence: console certification requires settings to survive user switches and to be available before sign-in (the XR/TRC user-profile rules). Lyra's shared-vs-local settings split shows the usual pattern: one settings registry that feeds the config layer.
- Proposed change: make persistence-save the single owner of the persisted player-settings store and add "settings registry (per scope)" to the `C-SAVE` summary, or add a separate `C-SETTINGS` (L3). Make the `C-CFG` user layer a read-only projection that this store populates. `C-A11YRT` settings register there. Add `C-SAVE` (or `C-SETTINGS`) to `consumes` of input-system, accessibility and runtime-scalability.

### K-GAMEPLAY-6 · major · omission
- Target: localization-i18n, text-fonts, C-LOC, C-TEXT, gameplay-data, gameplay-systems-toolkit, input-system (INP.ACT.glyphs), UI.LOC.*
- Finding: localization is not end to end.
  - (a) No capability covers message formatting with plural, gender, select and grammatical case. UI.LOC.cldr covers only numbers and dates, and the phrase "ICU-class message formatting" appears only in the skill purpose, so no critic or test ever sees it.
  - (b) No "localizable text" value type and no string gather exist. localization-i18n does not consume `C-REFL`/`C-SER`, and the skills that hold player-facing text in reflected data (gameplay-data tables, toolkit ability names, input glyph prompts, persistence slot names) do not consume `C-LOC`. Translatable text in data tables therefore cannot be gathered or kept stable.
  - (c) Locale never reaches shaping. `C-TEXT` has no language or script tag, and text-fonts does not consume `C-LOC` or `C-PAL`. That rules out correct Han-unification glyph selection (JA vs SC vs TC vs KO), dictionary line breaking for Thai/Lao/Khmer, and Japanese kinsoku, none of which UAX #14 alone covers.
- Evidence: Unicode MessageFormat 2.0 (CLDR 47, 2025), ICU plural and select rules, and UE FText gather from reflected properties in data assets. HarfBuzz and ICU need a BCP-47 language tag per run to select locl features and CJK variants.
- Proposed change: add the capabilities `UI.LOC.message-format` (MF2/ICU plural, gender, select, inflection; E) and `UI.LOC.gather` (localizable-text type in reflection plus gather from reflected and cooked assets; E), both owned by localization-i18n. Add `C-REFL` to localization-i18n `consumes`, and `C-LOC?` to gameplay-data, gameplay-systems-toolkit, input-system and persistence-save. Extend the `C-TEXT` summary with "runs carry BCP-47 language/script; locale-aware breaking". Add `UI.TXT.locale-shaping` to text-fonts, which receives the locale through `C-PAL` system events.

### K-GAMEPLAY-7 · major · dependency-error
- Target: C-DET, navigation-pathfinding, crowd-simulation, ai-behavior-perception, scripting-runtime, gameplay-systems-toolkit, gameplay-architect, gameplay-data; configs rts-2d-massim-client, rts-3d-massim-client
- Finding: `C-DET` is consumed by physics, network, prediction and character-movement, but by none of the gameplay-simulation skills. The two RTS massim configurations are `online`, and lockstep is the standard RTS model (NET.PRED.lockstep, NET.PRED.lockstep-replay). A lockstep simulation desyncs if pathfinding, flow fields, AI decisions (RNG, iteration order), script VMs (GC-dependent iteration, float paths), ability and effect evaluation, or tuning-data float parsing are non-deterministic. Rollback fighting games (GAM.SYS.hit-detection with prediction-rollback) face the same problem with toolkit effects. The cross-cutting "determinism" obligation asks each skill only to "declare" a level. No edge forces these skills to follow `C-DET` rules or be tested under it.
- Evidence: Age of Empires "1500 Archers on a 28.8" (Bettner/Terrano, GDC 2001), StarCraft II and Supreme Commander lockstep, and Factorio's desync reports. In each, desyncs are dominated by gameplay code, not physics.
- Proposed change: add `C-DET?` to navigation-pathfinding, crowd-simulation, ai-behavior-perception, scripting-runtime, gameplay-systems-toolkit, gameplay-architect and gameplay-data. Add a determinism-level declaration per skill to the rts configurations. Have `simulation-validation` run a desync-hash lane on rts-*-massim builds.

### K-GAMEPLAY-8 · major · overlap
- Target: GAM.AI.local-avoidance, navigation-pathfinding, crowd-simulation
- Finding: the capability GAM.AI.local-avoidance "Local avoidance (ORCA/RVO)" is owned by navigation-pathfinding. Both skill texts contradict this. Navigation's non-responsibilities say "Crowd avoidance → crowd-simulation", and crowd-simulation's purpose ("crowd avoidance") and expertise ("ORCA/RVO") claim it. Two agents will each believe that the other owns avoidance, or both will build it. The ownership also matters for scale-down: crowd-simulation only exists in `openworld`/`massim`, yet every 3D action game with a handful of NPCs needs avoidance.
- Evidence: van den Berg et al., "Reciprocal n-body Collision Avoidance" (ORCA), and Recast/Detour's DetourCrowd, where avoidance ships with the navigation library, not the mass-agent layer.
- Proposed change: keep the owner as navigation-pathfinding, which matches the needs of the smaller profiles. Remove the "Crowd avoidance" non-responsibility from navigation. Change crowd-simulation's purpose to "flow fields, mass-agent simulation and LOD, traffic; consumes C-NAV avoidance for individual agents". Remove ORCA/RVO from crowd expertise, or reword it as "mass-scale avoidance variants".

### K-GAMEPLAY-9 · major · overlap
- Target: ANM.ARCH.sync (animation-architect, "root-motion policy"), GAM.MOVE.root-motion (character-movement, "Root-motion vs capsule authority"), character-movement non_responsibilities
- Finding: two skills own root-motion policy. animation-architect owns "root-motion policy" inside ANM.ARCH.sync, and character-movement owns "root-motion vs capsule authority". These describe the same decision: whether the animation drives the capsule or the capsule drives the animation, and who is authoritative under prediction. Separately, character-movement lists "Animation selection → motion-synthesis". motion-synthesis is `std3d`-only, so in min2d and lite3d that non-responsibility points to a skill that is not built. Animation selection there belongs to animation-graphs.
- Evidence: the UE CharacterMovement/AnimInstance root-motion modes and the Ubisoft For Honor motion-matching and root-motion talks (GDC 2016/2018) show that one owner has to arbitrate authority. Split ownership produces double-applied root motion and server/client mismatch.
- Proposed change: remove "root-motion policy" from ANM.ARCH.sync, leaving "animation provides root-motion deltas and a trajectory via C-ANIM". Keep authority in GAM.MOVE.root-motion and add animation-architect as a contributor. Change the character-movement non-responsibility to "Animation selection → animation-graphs / motion-synthesis".

### K-GAMEPLAY-10 · major · dependency-error
- Target: motion-synthesis (consumes `C-MOVE?`), C-MOVE (L4), C-ANIM, ANM.SYN.trajectory
- Finding: motion-synthesis is an L3 animation module, but it consumes `C-MOVE`, which is L4 game framework. That is an upward link, and the gate misses it because motion-synthesis provides no contract and so has no "floor". The same blind spot covers every runtime skill with an empty `provides` (toolkit, crowd, cinematics, all animation and audio experts). Architecturally, the animation library would link against the gameplay movement module. The trajectory that motion matching needs should instead be pushed by character-movement through `C-ANIM` control input. The `C-ANIM` summary ("parameters, action/montage requests") has no trajectory or desired-velocity channel.
- Evidence: UE 5.4+ motion matching takes a trajectory as an anim-graph input that the movement component produces, so animation never depends on movement. For Honor (Clavet, GDC 2016) works the same way.
- Proposed change: remove `C-MOVE?` from motion-synthesis. Add "trajectory/desired-motion input, root-motion delta output" to the `C-ANIM` summary. Change check.py so a skill without `provides` takes its lead's contract layer as its floor, which catches the whole class.

### K-GAMEPLAY-11 · major · missing-contract
- Target: crowd-simulation
- Finding: besides lacking a provided contract (K-GAMEPLAY-2), crowd-simulation consumes only `C-NAV`, `C-ECS` and `C-SPATIAL`, which leaves it structurally disconnected in the massim configurations it exists for:
  - It does not consume `C-SIGNIF`, although GAM.AI.mass "Mass-agent simulation & LOD" is exactly the per-domain LOD policy that `C-SIGNIF` is defined to drive.
  - It does not consume `C-ANIM`/`C-INSTANCES`. ANM.DEF.crowd (VAT/animated impostors, contributor crowd-simulation) has no data path from crowd state.
  - It does not consume `C-AIAGENT`, so mass agents cannot make decisions.
  - GAM.AI.traffic lists vehicle-physics as a contributor, but crowd-simulation has no dependency that would let it promote an agent to a physics vehicle.
  - It does not consume `C-REP?`/`C-DET?`, although crowds in online games must be server-driven or deterministic.
- Evidence: in UE Mass, LOD comes from a significance/LOD processor, representation switches to ISM/VAT, and behavior comes from StateTree plus smart objects. The Cities: Skylines II and Total War postmortems describe the same wiring.
- Proposed change: add `C-SIGNIF`, `C-INSTANCES?`, `C-ANIM?`, `C-AIAGENT?`, `C-DET?` and `C-REP?` to crowd-simulation `consumes`, and add `C-CROWD` from K-GAMEPLAY-2. For traffic promotion, consume `C-PHYS?` and route the vehicle spawn through `C-GAME`.

### K-GAMEPLAY-12 · major · omission
- Target: capability map GAM/UI/RND; configs rts-2d-massim-client, rts-3d-massim-client, open-world-client
- Finding: no capability covers fog of war / team visibility, or maps (minimap, world map, compass, POI/marker registry). Both are staples of the RTS and open-world configurations the framework names. Fog of war spans gameplay (per-team vision computation, often on the GPU), rendering (fog overlay, explored state), replication (visibility-driven relevancy and anti-maphack, where lockstep is inherently vulnerable) and AI perception. Maps need a marker registry that gameplay, UI (UI.FW.world-ui) and render (map capture/tiles) share. Without an owner, each reference game will reinvent them across four skills.
- Evidence: StarCraft II and AoE IV fog/vision systems. Valorant's fog-of-war server-side visibility for anti-wallhack (Riot tech blog, 2020). The open-world map and marker systems in Ubisoft and Guerrilla titles.
- Proposed change: add `GAM.SYS.visibility` "Team visibility / fog of war (grid & GPU variants), explored state, visibility-driven relevancy hook" (owner gameplay-systems-toolkit; contributors replication, render-2d-vector, ai-behavior-perception; profile massim) and `GAM.SYS.markers` "POI/marker registry, minimap and world-map data" (owner gameplay-systems-toolkit; contributors ui-architect, render-architect). Add `C-REP` relevancy hook consumption for the visibility item.

### K-GAMEPLAY-13 · major · scale-down
- Target: animation-architect, animation-runtime, animation-graphs (profiles), cinematics-sequencer, config minimal-client
- Finding: the animation subtree is gated to `min2d/lite3d/std3d`, so `minimal-client` (card, UI and narrative games) has no animation runtime. These are exactly the games that rely on 2D skeletal and flipbook animation and on character rigs: visual novels (Live2D-class), card games (Spine rigs in Hearthstone- and Slay-the-Spire-class titles). `cinematics-sequencer` is in minimal but can animate nothing except UI. The "minimal" definition, "without a physics world", has been wrongly coupled to "without animation". Either UI.FW.animation grows a second 2D skeletal runtime, or the project jumps to min2d and pulls in physics.
- Evidence: Spine and Live2D runtimes are the dominant tech in UI- and narrative-centric commercial games. Both are purely 2D and physics-free.
- Proposed change: add `minimal` to the profiles of animation-architect, animation-runtime and animation-graphs. Tag 3D-only capabilities inside them (ANM.RT.compression ACL class, ANM.RT.pose-history) with capability-level profiles `lite3d`/`std3d`, so that minimal only carries ANM.RT.2d, ANM.RT.2d-import, blending and events.

### K-GAMEPLAY-14 · major · missing-contract
- Target: ui-architect, C-VIEW, C-UI, GAM.FW.local-players, UI.FW.focus, UI.FW.world-ui, configurations/milestones
- Finding: split-screen and local multiplayer are declared at gameplay (GAM.FW.local-players), render (RND.ARCH.multiview), audio (AUD.ARCH.listeners) and input (INP.ACT.local-mp), but UI does not consume `C-VIEW`. Add a local-multiplayer exit criterion to M1 or M2 (e.g. "2-player split-screen with independent UI focus, listeners and input assignment"), or add a `local-mp` add-on profile to one configuration.

### K-GAMEPLAY-15 · major · dependency-error
- Target: milestones.json M1 (`C-UI` frozen), C-TEXT, C-LOC, C-INPUT, C-REFL, C-GAME, C-SAVE, C-DIALOGUE, C-GAMEDATA, C-MOVE, C-A11YRT, C-SCRIPT
- Finding: `C-UI` is frozen at M1 (a 2D, PC-only slice), yet its `requires` (`C-TEXT`, `C-LOC`, `C-INPUT`, `C-REFL`) are never frozen at any milestone. `C-UI` is frozen before its first consumers of world-space UI (M2), XR interaction (M5) and console platform dialogs exist. None of the game-facing contracts (`C-GAME`, `C-INPUT`, `C-SAVE`, `C-LOC`, `C-TEXT`, `C-DIALOGUE`, `C-GAMEDATA`, `C-MOVE`, `C-SCRIPT`, `C-A11YRT`, `C-NAV`) is ever frozen. Game teams, and the reference-game agents at every rung, therefore build against contracts that may still change underneath them through M5, while one contract is frozen too early.
- Evidence: a frozen contract whose required contracts can still change is not actually frozen. This is the general rule of API stability under C-API. Freezing is only meaningful once the widest consumer set has exercised the contract.
- Proposed change: move the `C-UI` freeze to M2, with `C-TEXT`, `C-LOC`, `C-INPUT` and `C-REFL` frozen at or before it. Freeze `C-GAME`, `C-SAVE`, `C-GAMEDATA`, `C-DIALOGUE`, `C-A11YRT` and `C-SCRIPT` at M2, `C-MOVE`/`C-NAV` at M4, after prediction. Add a check that no frozen contract requires an unfrozen or later-frozen contract, and that every non-P contract is frozen by the final milestone.

### K-GAMEPLAY-17 · major · wrong-boundary
- Target: gameplay-systems-toolkit (GAM.SYS.camera, GAM.SYS.photo, GAM.SYS.building)
- Finding: the toolkit is a 12-capability grab bag that mixes unrelated literatures: ability/effect systems, fighting-game frame data, player construction with "structural integrity" (which overlaps destruction-fracture's structural simulation), and the gameplay camera. Under the framework's own split rule ("different literatures, validated separately", 00 §1), the camera is the clearest candidate for its own skill. It is on the critical path of every game. It has its own literature (Haigh-Hutchinson, "Real-Time Cameras"; GDC "50 Game Camera Mistakes", Nystrom/Kircher). It couples to a11y motion options, XR comfort, split-screen and cinematics blending. It is also validated separately, through motion-sickness, occlusion and framing tests. As things stand, the ability-system agent owns camera feel.
- Evidence: UE's GameplayCameras (5.5) is a separate module from GAS. Cinemachine is a separate package in Unity. Structural integrity sits with physics and voxel systems (e.g. Fortnite's building versus Teardown-class structural simulation).
- Proposed change: create an expert `gameplay-camera` under gameplay-architect that owns GAM.SYS.camera and GAM.SYS.photo. It consumes `C-VIEW`, `C-PHYS?` (collision), `C-INPUT?`, `C-A11YRT` and `C-GAME`, with 2D camera (dead-zones, parallax, pixel snapping) as an explicit capability. Move "structural integrity" out of GAM.SYS.building to destruction-fracture as a contributor-owned sub-capability, or name destruction-fracture as the owner of the stability solver.

### K-GAMEPLAY-19 · minor · maturity-error
- Target: GAM.AI.llm, radar "Learned & LLM-driven agents", docs/06 A12
- Finding: GAM.AI.llm is labeled `M` and the radar groups it with learned policies (GT Sophy). Gap analysis A12, however, calls LLM-driven NPCs "`experimental`". The labels contradict each other.
