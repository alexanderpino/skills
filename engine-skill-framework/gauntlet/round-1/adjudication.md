Scope: the 13 partially accepted findings in `for-adjudication.md`, judged against the current `data/*.json` and `docs/00-design-principles.md`. `scripts/check.py` reports 0 errors and 0 warnings (1154 capabilities, 143 skills, 100 contracts, 15 critics).

### K-COMPLETE-3 · UPHOLD
- Verified in data: `PLAT.PAL.process` now reads "... HTTP(S)/TLS client (platform stacks)" (owner platform-architect, contributor security-engineering), and the `C-PAL` summary names "HTTP(S)/TLS client". `NET.TRANS.web` covers WebSocket and WebRTC data channels, with WebTransport where available. `CORE.TYPES.crypto` ("Vetted cryptographic primitives wrapper, CSPRNG, per-platform selection", containers-core-types, contributor security-engineering) is exposed through `C-TYPES`. `XC.SEC.crypto-policy` ("Cryptography & TLS/certificate policy", security-engineering) exists. Adjacent concerns: `RES.IO.remote` (HTTP/CDN IO with cache and offline policy), `RES.IO.errors` (retries) and `BLD.REL.on-demand` (background assets).
- Ruling rationale: The HTTPS/TLS client, the browser transports, the crypto primitives with CSPRNG and the TLS/certificate policy each now have exactly one owner and a contract path. Putting the client in the PAL is technically sound, because console certification requires the platform network stacks. Keeping primitives in a foundation (L1) skill, with policy owned by security, follows the §4 policy-vs-implementation pattern. Non-binding note: the HTTP client is a clause inside a capability about processes and pipes, and proxies and certificate pinning are not named anywhere. Splitting it into its own `PLAT.PAL.http` line would help phase-2 research seeding, but the ownership gap the critic raised is closed.

### K-QUALITY-6 · OVERTURN
- Verified in data: `QA.STRAT.contracts` is narrowed to "Contract-test framework & consumer-driven test policy". `C-TEST` now includes "conformance suites owned by contract owners with consumer-driven additions". All 86 non-P contracts carry `"conformance": true`. However, `scripts/check.py` and `scripts/model.py` contain no reference to `conformance` (grep returns nothing). The flag is therefore not checked, although the disposition says "(checked)". `C-ORCH` contains no rule that a provider change must pass the consumer-contributed tests.
- Ruling rationale: Ownership is resolved in substance, and a separate owner field would indeed be redundant. But one of the two claimed changes is missing. With no guard, a flag that is always `true` carries no information, and a new contract can omit it silently. The critic's point about replaceability also depends on a provider change being gated by consumer-driven tests, and that gating rule is not stated.
- Required change:
  1. In `check.py`, add `E(f"code contract {cid} has no conformance suite")` for every contract with layer ≠ "P" that lacks `conformance: true`. Add a mutation case for it to `selftest()`.
  2. Extend the `C-TEST` or `C-ORCH` summary with "a provider change merges only when the contract's conformance suite, including consumer-contributed cases, passes".

### K-SYSTEMS-13 · OVERTURN
- Verified in data: `CORE.SCALE.governor` and `CORE.SCALE.actuators` exist under runtime-scalability. `PLAT.PAL.power` ("Power, thermal & performance-hint APIs", platform-architect, contributor runtime-scalability) exists. The runtime-scalability `expertise` includes ADPF and Apple thermal state. Two gaps remain:
  - `PLAT.MOB.thermal` still reads "Thermal & power adaptivity". It is owned by platform-mobile, a runtime skill whose `purpose` still says "thermal/power adaptivity".
  - The registered actuators are "dynres, significance, VFX, animation rate, workers". Frame-rate cap and refresh-rate selection are not among them, and neither is P/E-core placement. Frame-rate and refresh-rate selection live in `PLAT.MOB.frame-pacing` and CORE.FRAME, and placement lives in `CORE.JOBS.priorities`. job-system-task-graph is not a contributor to `CORE.SCALE.actuators`.

The critic's concrete failure mode was "dynres, job placement and thermal handling run three uncoordinated control loops". The data still gives a second runtime skill a capability worded as thermal adaptation, so two agents can each legitimately build a thermal control loop. The frame-rate cap, the primary thermal actuator in ADPF guidance and on handhelds, and P/E placement, both of which the critic named, are not registered as governor actuators.
- Required change:
  1. Rename `PLAT.MOB.thermal` to "Mobile thermal/power signal backends (ADPF thermal headroom, performance-hint sessions) feeding CORE.SCALE.governor; no independent control loop", and update the platform-mobile `purpose` to match.
  2. Change `CORE.SCALE.actuators` to "(frame-rate cap/refresh selection, dynres, significance, VFX, animation rate, worker count & P/E placement)". Add frame-orchestration, platform-mobile and job-system-task-graph as contributors.
